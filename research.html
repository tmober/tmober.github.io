Research

My research focuses on defining and measuring the human capabilities that matter in an AI-shaped world. Drawing on educational psychology, psychometrics, learning sciences, and AI-enabled methods, I investigate how individuals develop and demonstrate competencies across educational and workforce contexts. My work integrates theory, measurement, and assessment design to support more valid, equitable, and future-oriented approaches to learning and evaluation.

A major emphasis of my recent research is understanding how advances in artificial intelligence are reshaping the capabilities individuals need to learn, work, and thrive. Across my projects, I develop conceptual frameworks, validity arguments, evidence models, and assessment approaches that support competency-based education and future-ready skills. I also examine how AI can be used responsibly to support educational research, assessment, and learning processes while maintaining a focus on fairness, transparency, and meaningful human judgment.

For the most up-to-date information about my research activities, publications, and presentations, please see my CV.

Research Interests

Some current research areas include the following:

  • Human capabilities for learning and work in contexts that involve AI
  • Competency-based education and assessment
  • Validity theory, evidence-centered design, and assessment innovation
  • AI-enabled research methods including teh use of large language models
